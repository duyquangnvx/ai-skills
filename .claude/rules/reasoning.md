Judge feasibility by what you can actually execute: if you can reliably perform or support it, treat it as feasible. Not matching a traditional implementation pattern is not a reason to dismiss it.

**Build for where models are heading, not just where they are now.** Scaffolding that only compensates for a current model weakness is a depreciating asset — keep it thin and easy to rip out. What survives model upgrades is data and tool access, evals, observability, UX, and safety; invest there. Shipping at 80% of today's ceiling beats hard-coding around a gap the next release closes.

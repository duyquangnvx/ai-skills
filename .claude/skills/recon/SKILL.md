An idea has arrived without a shape. There is nothing to sharpen yet, because the directions it could take aren't named. Recon goes and finds out: send scouts, come back with a map, hand the choice over. Scouts don't engage — recon never descends into the direction it finds.

## Name the axes first

Before any scout leaves, state the **axes** of this space — the dimensions the directions differ along, read off this problem rather than a checklist. The axes of an architecture question are not the axes of a market question or a content question.

Put them to the user and wait. Axes are the cheapest thing in the session to correct and the most expensive to get wrong: every scout runs down them.

## Send scouts, one framing each

A single context anchors on the first idea it produces, and everything after is a variant of that idea. Breadth comes from independent contexts, so every candidate direction gets its own subagent, briefed on one framing and sent to find evidence for it — in this codebase, in the docs, in the wider world, wherever that framing would leave traces.

Brief scouts to compete. A scout makes its framing as strong as the evidence allows and reports what it found, not what it assumed.

Include the uncomfortable framings — the one where the problem is somewhere else entirely, the one where this is already solved and the work is adoption, the one where doing nothing wins.

## The landscape

Report every scout in the same shape, so the directions read across rather than down:

```
🧭 **<direction>** — <one line: what taking this way means>

**Ground** — what the scouts found: what already exists, who has walked this way, what it cost them.
**Kills it** — the thing that ends this direction. Every direction has one; a direction with none was scouted badly.
**Fog** — what stays unknown until someone commits.
```

Below the map: the way in you'd take, and why.

A landscape whose every entry the user could have written themselves is a failed recon. That is the bar the scouts are held to, and the reason the fan-out is worth its cost.

## Stop at the choice

Recon is done when the user has picked a way in, or has redrawn the space and sent the scouts back out.

The map is the deliverable. Sharpening the chosen way into a decision is a separate session with a separate job — hand the map over and stop there.

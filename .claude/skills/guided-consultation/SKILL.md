# Guided Consultation

Users rarely know what they want in the abstract, but they react well to a concrete proposal. So surface one **branch** at a time, attach a **straw man** the user can accept or knock down, and produce the deliverable as soon as a useful first version is possible.

## Produce early

The deliverable beats one more question. Produce as soon as any of these holds:

- There is enough context for a useful first version.
- The remaining unknowns are minor, or easier to settle by reacting to a draft.
- The user shows impatience, asks you to choose, or asks to skip ahead.
- You have asked 4-5 questions in the current phase without producing anything concrete.

Label assumptions inline, only where they change the result.

## The loop

1. **Orient, then ask.** One or two sentences: restate the goal and name the branches you will cover. Ask the first branch in the same message.

2. **One branch per message.** A branch may carry 2-3 options; budget, timeline and style are three branches, not one message. Use multiple choice where the answer space is closed, open questions only where it is genuinely open, such as budget.

3. **Straw man before asking**, whenever a defensible default exists: frame the trade-off in one sentence, give the recommendation and one reason, then ask. Where the branch is pure taste, say so and just ask — a manufactured recommendation is worse than none.

   Trade-off branch:

   ```markdown
   <One sentence framing the trade-off.>
   I recommend <choice> because <reason>.

   Which direction fits?

   - A) <recommended option>
   - B) <alternative>
   - C) <alternative or "something else">
   ```

   Taste branch:

   ```markdown
   This is mostly taste, so I would not force a recommendation.

   Which direction sounds better?

   - A) <option>
   - B) <option>
   - C) Something else
   ```

   Open constraint:

   ```markdown
   The next constraint shapes the whole plan: <constraint>.

   What range should I assume?
   ```

4. **Take the answer and move to the highest-impact remaining unknown.** A knocked-down straw man is the loop working: adopt the user's preference and drop the reasoning that pointed elsewhere.

5. **Mirror after 2-4 branches.** Summarize the direction so far and ask whether it is right, then continue or produce.

## Depth

Size the consultation silently before the first message:

- **Low stakes, few branches** — 1-2 questions, then produce.
- **Medium** — 3-6 questions with one mirror at the midpoint.
- **High stakes or many branches** — named phases, a mirror closing each phase, and something concrete produced within each phase. The 4-5 question ceiling applies per phase, not to the consultation as a whole.

For high-stakes financial, legal, medical or safety decisions, slow down and ask for the missing critical constraint rather than assuming it.

## Adaptation

- Rich, contextful answers — drop the multiple-choice scaffolding and respond naturally.
- An answer that reshapes the task — re-scope out loud in one sentence.
- "Just tell me what you would do" — best-guess draft with assumptions.
- The task turns out factual or fully specified — stop consulting and execute it.

## Examples

<example>
Opening a consultation.

User: "Help me plan a weekend in Tokyo."

> Happy to. I will narrow this by pace, interests, and budget, then draft a weekend itinerary.
>
> For a weekend, the first branch is depth versus coverage. I recommend 2 neighborhoods in depth, because cross-city travel eats too much of a short trip.
>
> Which pace fits?
>
> - A) 2 neighborhoods, deeper
> - B) 3-4 highlights across the city
> - C) Pick for me after I share interests

</example>

<example>
Stopping. Three branches are settled — warm tone, no reason given, two weeks' notice — and what is left (exact date, manager's name) is minor.

> That is enough to draft. Here is the letter, assuming a last day of the 28th — say the word and I will shift it.
>
> [the letter]

</example>
